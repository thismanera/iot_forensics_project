Here is a recommended, step-by-step workflow to tackle your project's development.

### **Phase 1: The Theoretical Foundation (Weeks 1-2)**
Before touching any code or virtual machines, you need to establish the ground truth. This will form the "Theoretical Part" of your final report.

* **Review NIST Guidelines:** Start with **NIST SP 800-183** (Networks of 'Things') and **NISTIR 8259** (Foundational Cybersecurity Activities for IoT Device Manufacturers). Look specifically for what network behaviors are *expected* versus *anomalous*.
* **Research Traffic Fingerprinting:** Read up on how encrypted IoT traffic is analyzed. Since payloads are usually encrypted (TLS/SSL), investigators rely on metadata: packet lengths, inter-arrival times (IAT), burst sizes, and DNS queries. 
* **Analyze Existing Frameworks:** Look into conceptual models like the *FDIoT (Forensic Data-driven framework for IoT)* or *IoT-Roam*. Understand their limitations so you can position your methodology as a practical solution.

### **Phase 2: Environment Design & Setup (Weeks 2-3)**
Your professor noted that Mininet-IoT can be tricky to configure. The goal here is a stable environment, not necessarily the most complex one. 

* **Choose Your Hypervisor/Simulator:** * *Option A (The Academic Standard):* **Mininet-WiFi / Mininet-IoT**. Great if you can get it running, but documentation can be sparse.
    * *Option B (The Pragmatic Approach):* Standard **Mininet** or **GNS3**, using lightweight Docker containers or Python scripts to simulate the *network behavior* of IoT devices (e.g., a script that pings an MQTT broker every 5 seconds to simulate a temperature sensor).
* **Define the Topology:** Keep it simple. Create a simulated home router/gateway, an external cloud server (for the IoT devices to phone home to), and 3-4 distinct IoT devices (e.g., a smart bulb, an IP camera, and a smart plug).
* **Establish Baseline Behaviors:** Map out what each device does normally. 
    * *Camera:* High bandwidth upload, constant TCP stream.
    * *Smart Plug:* Low bandwidth, intermittent MQTT/HTTP POST requests.

### **Phase 3: Data Generation & Capture (Weeks 4-5)**
This is where you build your "experimental dataset."

* **Capture Normal Traffic:** Run your simulation in its normal state for an extended period (e.g., 24 virtual hours). Use `tcpdump` or **Wireshark** at the gateway node to capture all traffic into a baseline PCAP file.
* **Simulate Attacks & Anomalies:** * *Unauthorized Access:* Simulate an SSH brute force or default-credential login to the IP camera.
    * *DDoS/Botnet:* Simulate a Mirai-like infection where an IoT device suddenly starts scanning external IP addresses or flooding a target.
    * *Data Exfiltration:* A device sending larger-than-normal payloads to an unknown IP.
* **Tag the Data:** Document exactly *when* (timestamps) you executed the attacks. This is crucial for verifying the accuracy of your forensic timeline later.

### **Phase 4: Forensic Analysis & Feature Extraction (Weeks 5-6)**
Now, put on your investigator hat. You will process the PCAP files to see if you can blindly identify what happened.

* **Protocol Analysis with Zeek:** Feed your PCAPs into **Zeek** (formerly Bro). Zeek is perfect for this because it automatically parses traffic into highly readable, forensics-friendly logs (`conn.log`, `dns.log`, `ssl.log`).
* **Identify Behavioral Features:** Analyze the logs to answer the core question: *Which network features best identify device behavior?* Look for:
    * Specific port usage (e.g., 1883 for MQTT, 5683 for CoAP).
    * Volume of bytes transferred in a given window.
    * DNS requests to specific vendor domains.
* **Reconstruct the Timeline:** Use your findings to build a narrative. (e.g., "At 14:02, the device connected to an unauthorized IP; at 14:05, an anomalous outbound traffic spike occurred, indicating exfiltration.") Compare this timeline against your notes from Phase 3 to measure accuracy.

### **Phase 5: Synthesis and Deliverables (Weeks 7-8)**
Consolidate your findings into the final project requirements.

* **The Methodology:** Write down the step-by-step process an investigator should follow when handed a PCAP from an IoT network.
* **Behavioral Classification:** Create a taxonomy or table classifying the devices based on the network features you identified (e.g., High-Volume/Continuous vs. Low-Volume/Event-Driven).
* **Package the Dataset:** Clean your PCAPs and Zeek logs, add a README, and package them as your contribution to IoT forensic research.
